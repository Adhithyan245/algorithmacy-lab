## Problems found

Each fix is a proposal for the author. I edited nothing.

1. **Grudin durations (REVIEW.md l. 288–289).** Old: "its own "duration of basic events studied," running from milliseconds to days." New: "its own "duration of basic events studied," running from microseconds at the hardware level to days at the work setting." Source: Grudin (1990), Table 1, p. 265 (CHI '90 VoR): Microseconds/hours · Milliseconds/hours · Seconds · Minutes · Days. The error probably comes from facet F4's thematic synthesis, which misreads the same row ("milliseconds for the terminal").
2. **Grudin disciplines (l. 287).** Old: "social psychology, anthropology and organizational science". New: "social psychology, anthropology and organizational …" or "social psychology, anthropology and organizational studies [Grudin's cell is truncated]". Source: Table 1, p. 265 reads "Social psych., anthropology, organizational...".
3. **Grudin "criteria" (l. 299–302).** Old: "A decision committed through an algorithmic intermediary meets each of Grudin's criteria for a new level: its events run from days to months…". Suggested: "A decision committed through an algorithmic intermediary differs along the rows of Grudin's table: its events run…". Grudin states distinctions, not admission criteria (Table 1 caption "Summary of the distinctions across levels", p. 265; "Each level … has its own practitioners. Each relies on a science base that is less mature than the previous level", p. 266). Author's call: "days to months" matches Grudin's level 5 ("Days"; social processes "unfold over weeks or months", p. 265), so the duration row supports a continuation of level 5 more than a new level.
4. **Itera citing "the others" (l. 32–33).** Old: "Only one of them, the Chilean consultancy Itera, cites the others, and Itera concedes…". New: "Only one of them, the Chilean consultancy Itera, cites any of the others — Magine Pro's streaming model and Andrew Audry — and it concedes…". Source: itera.cl page (2025-07-04), paragraph "Decision Experience: la UX de las decisiones".
5. **Kalantzis & Cope misquote (l. 503).** Old: "generative AI is "before anything else a technology of writing,"". New: "generative AI is "more than anything a technology of writing,"". Source: RRQ 60(1) e591, abstract, first sentence.
6. **Klawitter & Hargittai context (l. 489–490).** Old: "whose informant called selling through a ranked marketplace "like learning a whole other language."" New: "whose informant, a knitwear seller struggling with Instagram hashtags, called it "like learning a whole other language."" Source: IJoC 12, pp. 3499–3500.
7. **Hollender et al. count (l. 279–281).** Old: "found 65 ACM publications applying cognitive load theory to interfaces". New: "found 65 publications in the ACM Guide to Computing Literature with "cognitive load" in their titles or abstracts and concluded that CLT concepts had been adopted in HCI". Source: abstract (Consensus record of 10.1016/j.chb.2010.05.031).
8. **Lai et al. count (l. 421–422).** Old: "coded more than 80 empirical studies". Either: "surveyed more than 100 papers" (the FAccT version's abstract, which is the version the bib cites), or keep "more than 80 in-scope studies" and cite arXiv:2112.11471v1 §2 as the version read. The §6 quote was verified in arXiv v1 only.

New (for Li): "Li and colleagues (2024) define designers' AI literacy as understanding how AI works and questioning its generated outputs". Drop the quotation marks unless the phrase is verbatim in Alvarez (2026). Source: Li et al. §6.3 (ir.cwi.nl OA copy).
