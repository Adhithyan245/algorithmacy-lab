## Problems found

**P1. Gray et al. (2018) did not name dark patterns.** (REVIEW.md l. 142)
- Old: "Gray and colleagues (2018) named *dark patterns* — interface designs that turn the architect's knowledge against the user —"
- New: "Gray and colleagues (2018) gave *dark patterns*, a practitioner's term, an academic definition — interface designs that turn the architect's knowledge against the user —"
- Source: repo card `submissions/algorithmacy_design_ethics/literature/library/gray2018.md` (read in full): "The authors take the practitioner term coined by Harry Brignull in 2010 and give it an academic definition" (p. 1). Brignull is not in the review's references, so this wording avoids adding a new citation.

**P2. Doumont (2002) argued the point but did not document it.** (REVIEW.md l. 306)
- Old: "Doumont (2002) documented practitioners citing Miller unread"
- New: "Doumont (2002) argued that practitioners cite Miller without having read him"
- Source: abstract (OpenAlex, 10.1109/tpc.2002.1003695): "Equally likely, however, he or she will never have read this article and will happily go on quoting it out of context." The paper is an essay, not a survey, and nobody read beyond the abstract (F4).

**P3. The "jagged frontier" quote does not match the only text read.** (REVIEW.md l. 366)
- Old: `Consultants working inside the "jagged frontier" of AI capability completed 12.2% more tasks`
- New (option a, verbatim): `Consultants working inside the "jagged technology frontier" of AI capability completed 12.2% more tasks`
- New (option b, unquoted): `Consultants working inside the jagged frontier of AI capability completed 12.2% more tasks`
- Source: *Organization Science* abstract (Crossref JATS, 10.1287/orsc.2025.21838): "We introduce and study the concept of a 'jagged technology frontier'". The two-word form may appear in the full text, but nobody has read it (F5 #34: abstract only).
