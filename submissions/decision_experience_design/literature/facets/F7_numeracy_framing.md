## Entries

**peters2006numeracy** — see F6 (Crossref record; abstract only; paywalled, no OA copy found this
session). Carries the independence-from-intelligence claim only at abstract level: "the effect of
numeracy was not due to general intelligence."

**reyna2023numeracy**
Reyna, V. F., & Brainerd, C. J. (2023). Numeracy, gist, literal thinking and the value of nothing in
decision making. *Nature Reviews Psychology, 2*(7), 421–439. https://doi.org/10.1038/s44159-023-00188-7
- Basis: Crossref record; full text via Europe PMC (PMC10196318), read this session.
- Read status: **read in full** (targeted: abstract, §"Objective and subjective numeracy",
  §"Summary and future directions", reference list).
- Numerical competencies "predict informed and accurate risky decision making in business and
  engineering …, medicine and health communication … and civil and criminal law" (§"Objective and
  subjective numeracy"). "Numeracy predicts an impressive array of life outcomes in such critical
  areas as health (perceptions of risk, quality of health decisions, patient outcomes), finance
  (employment, retirement savings, wealth) and law (sentencing decisions, civil damage awards)"
  (§"Summary and future directions"). The abstract argues that "a literal focus on objective numbers
  and mechanical number crunching is misguided": rote, verbatim processing "treat[s] numbers as data
  as opposed to information," whereas gist extraction organizes them meaningfully, and "gist training
  facilitates transfer to new contexts and, because it is more durable, longer-lasting improvements
  in decision making."
- **Role in the DXD argument:** precursor — the numeracy template for algorithmacy: a competence
  that predicts decision quality across domains, and whose skilled form is gist (meaning in context),
  not verbatim reading. The verbatim/gist split is a parallel to the legibility ruling (a user who
  reads the algorithm's rule verbatim has data, not information); the parallel is this review's
  inference, not Reyna and Brainerd's claim.

**sobkow2020multiple**
Sobkow, A., Olszewska, A., & Traczyk, J. (2020). Multiple numeric competencies predict decision
outcomes beyond fluid intelligence and cognitive reflection. *Intelligence, 80*, 101452.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.intell.2020.101452
- Basis: Crossref record; OpenAlex abstract (OA hybrid copy exists, not read).
- Read status: **abstract only**.
- In a large general-population sample, "multiple numeric competencies predicted decision making
  beyond fluid intelligence and cognitive reflection"; approximate numeracy ("the acuity of
  symbolic-number mapping") was "the most robust single predictor of superior decision making"
  (abstract).
- **Role in the DXD argument:** precursor — the independence-from-general-ability evidence at a
  later date and with more controls than Peters et al. (2006); the model for the incremental-validity
  test an algorithmacy measure must pass.

## Synthesis

The numeracy literature supplies the argument form the author chose: an "-acy" competence earns its
name when it predicts decision quality over and above general ability (Peters et al., 2006; Sobkow
et al., 2020) across consequential domains (Reyna & Brainerd, 2023). That is an empirical claim with
a known test — incremental validity over fluid intelligence and cognitive reflection — and it does
not require the claim that the medium restructures thought. Reyna and Brainerd add a second point the
DXD argument can use with care: skilled numeracy is gist, not verbatim computation, and training for
gist transfers and lasts. If algorithmacy has the same shape, the skilled form is not reading the
intermediary's rule more exactly, which fits the design-ethics ruling. That parallel is untested.
